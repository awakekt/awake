- **The engine's own texture loaders upload to the GPU without a readback on the web.** glTF models (base colour and every PBR map), scene sprite sheets and tilemap atlases that `AssetSourceTextureResolver` loads, and particle sprites now decode with `decodeImage` (`awake:core:image`), which a browser keeps as an `ImageBitmap`. Their materials are made with `createImageMaterial`, so WebGPU copies the images on the GPU and every other renderer uploads their CPU pixels as before (#1184).
  - On headless Chromium, making a glTF material with two 1024×1024 maps now holds the main thread for about 6–7 ms, where the CPU path took about 390–450 ms.
  - New API, all additive:
    - `DecodedImage`, `decodeImage` and `Bitmap.asDecodedImage`;
    - `PbrImageSet`, plus `asGpuImageSource` for a `DecodedImage` or a single-layer `TextureAsset`;
    - `SceneTextureLoad.Loaded(source)` and `SceneAssetLibrary.textureImage`;
    - image-taking constructors on `SpriteRenderBatch`, `TilemapRenderBatch` and `ParticleContentSystem`;
    - `loadParticleImages`, `CoreSceneContent.ParticleImages` and `GltfAssetResolver.createMaterial(renderer, name)`.
  - Behaviour change for hosts: a project's loaded content now holds particle sprites under `ParticleImages`. A host's own `ParticleSprites` still works, but an emitter takes an image from `ParticleImages` first.
  - Changed since 0.30.0, in API that has not been released: `createImageMaterial` and `GpuImageUpload.createMaterial` take a `PbrImageSet`, and `BrowserImage.asGpuImageSource()` (`awake:backend:webgpu`) is replaced by `DecodedImage.asGpuImageSource()`.
