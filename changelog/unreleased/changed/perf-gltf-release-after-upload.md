- **A played project's glTF models let go of their CPU copies once uploaded.** The glTF resolver kept every static mesh's geometry and every material's image file bytes for its whole life, so that a mesh or material released and needed again could be made again: 27 MiB of live heap in a populated scene a consumer measured on its way to Android's heap limit (#1753). `GltfAssetResolver.releaseAfterUpload(path)` lets a model's static geometry go once its mesh is made and its images go once its material is made (`GpuImageSource.releaseImageData`), and a mesh or material made again reads the model again first, through `loadMesh` and `loadMaterial`, the async path a scene makes its meshes through. The project runtime marks the models its scene preloads, and models read through `loadModel` are released the same way; `keepAfterUpload(path)` keeps them for a host that reads them itself, such as for picking or collision. A model with levels of detail keeps its geometry, which the levels share. Making a released mesh again synchronously, with no load first, such as through `runtime.requireMesh` or a synchronous `spawn` after its GPU mesh was destroyed, fails with a message saying to load it, and a released material made that way draws untextured.
